Why localisation, not translation

Italy is one of the largest e-commerce markets in the EU, and most Italian shoppers read product pages in Italian even when they understand English. A word-for-word translation sounds foreign and hurts trust at checkout. Localisation means the text reads as if it had been written in Italian: sizes and units converted, shipping and returns wording adapted to what Italian customers expect, the tone kept (friendly, premium, technical, playful) and the calls to action phrased the way Italian stores phrase them.

An example written for this page (a fictional product, so nothing here belongs to a client):

English source

Harbour Wool Beanie
Knitted from 100% merino wool in a chunky rib, the Harbour beanie keeps its shape wash after wash. One size fits most. Free UK shipping on orders over £50; 30-day returns.

Italian localisation

Berretto Harbour in lana
Lavorato a coste larghe in pura lana merino, il berretto Harbour mantiene la forma lavaggio dopo lavaggio. Taglia unica. Spedizione gratuita in Italia per ordini superiori a 60 €; reso entro 30 giorni.

What changed, besides the words: the shipping threshold is shown in euro for the Italian market (you tell me the real one), the currency symbol goes after the amount with a space, as Italian stores write it, and "one size fits most" becomes the standard Italian label "taglia unica".
